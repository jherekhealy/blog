Burley Scrambling Beyond the Fourth Dimension – The Return

 Posted on August 25, 2026  |  Jherek Healy

Yesterday, I revisited how to extend Burley scrambling beyond four dimensions see previous post.

I favored the implementation suggested by Burley himself in his paper: padding by 4D blocks. I also presented an alternative construction, partly used in QuantLib, which relies on a classic high-dimensional direction-vector table and applies the Burley/Laine–Karras-based scrambling on top of it.

The 4D-by-4D padding approach is attractive because it allows effectively unlimited dimensions while preserving good stratification within each 4D block. However, I found examples where this “classic” Burley implementation performs poorly.

A very basic test case is computing the expectation of a geometric Brownian motion using an incremental time-step construction. In this setup, both the error and the standard error of the simulation are significantly higher with the classic Burley implementation than with the high-dimensional direction (HDD) Burley variant. The standard error of the HDD variant is in line with what one observes for more classic scramblings, such as a digital shift or ACM-823.

For high-dimensional integration, as we routinely do in quantitative finance, the lesson is clear: avoid the 4D-padded variant (4DP) Burley construction in favor of the HDD variant (or other well-tested high-dimensional direction tables with proper scrambling). I wonder if Peter Caspers, who contributed the Burley 2020 scrambled Sobol generator to QuantLib, ran similar tests.

Below is a small excerpt from my results for a 10-dimensional GBM expectation estimate with \(N = 32{,}768\) points and 32 replicates:

dimensions scrambling N replicates estimator rqmc_se replicate_sd elapsed
10 BURLEY-4DP 32768 32 0.999066216 7.0843e-04 4.0075e-03 0.8660888672
10 BURLEY-HDD 32768 32 1.000108172 1.2582e-04 7.1176e-04 0.8678109646

The first row corresponds to the classic high-dimensional Burley construction; the second uses the 4D-padded / HDD-style variant. The reduction in both rqmc_se and replicate_sd is substantial, even though the raw timings are similar.
